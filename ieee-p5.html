Project 5 — Photoresistor

PhotoresistorADCVoltage DividerESP32
What I Built

A light-sensing circuit using a photoresistor in a voltage divider. The ESP32's ADC reads the divided voltage and outputs a value between 0 and 4095 representing light intensity. Tested the circuit across different ambient lighting conditions to find the useful reading range.

Why a Voltage Divider

A photoresistor doesn't produce a voltage on its own. It just changes its resistance based on how much light hits it. To get a readable signal, you wire it in series with a fixed resistor. As the photoresistor's resistance shifts, the voltage at the midpoint between the two changes. That midpoint is what the ADC reads. Without the companion resistor, the ADC pin would float and read nothing useful.

Finding the Range

Ran tests under different conditions: bright light directly on the sensor, normal room lighting, and covering the sensor completely. Mapped those to ADC readings and used those values to set thresholds for triggering a response. The midpoint reading between minimum and maximum was where the most sensitive detection happened.
